Reflection

Ticket 101: The live path matched the expected Boss → Inventory route and then added Customer Service. Inventory verified 0 size-S tees and the open vendor invoice, while Customer Service drafted a response without promising delivery. The ticket stayed open because the current tools cannot clear the invoice or fulfill shipping.

Ticket 102: The expected plan anticipated a Facilities → Accounting handoff. The actual live run stopped after Facilities returned a structured $2,400 proposal, so no second Accounting handoff was needed. The proposal remained gated until a desk operator approved it; after approval, the payment table recorded the transaction, cash became $1,000, and the ticket became resolved.

Ticket 103: The expected plan anticipated an Inventory → Accounting margin review. The actual live run used Inventory → Customer Service: Inventory verified 8 available for 20 requested and the vendor constraint, then Customer Service drafted a careful response. No unsupported discount was approved, so the ticket stayed open.

A single agent would be simpler for ticket 102 because it mainly needs one lease lookup and one payment proposal. The actual differences show why delegation needs to remain flexible: Customer Service was more useful than Accounting for the customer-facing parts of 101 and 103, while Facilities' structured proposal made an extra Accounting handoff unnecessary for 102.

Three new problems this team can solve are a mug restock, a cap availability question, and a routine rent reminder. Three problems these tools cannot solve are accepting a credit-card payment through a processor, shipping a package with fulfillment tracking, and handling a damaged-item photo through returns intake. I would add Payment, Fulfillment, and Returns agents plus those tools.
